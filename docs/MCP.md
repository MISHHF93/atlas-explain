# ATLAS Explain for AI assistants (MCP)

ATLAS Explain has a small, read-only [Model Context Protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) server that is hosted online. People add one web address to an AI assistant that supports MCP connectors (Claude, and others), and the assistant can look things up in ATLAS Explain while they chat. Nothing is downloaded or installed, and it needs no account or key.

## What it can do

| Tool | What it returns |
|---|---|
| `explain_term` | The plain-language glossary entry for a market term, such as "stop-limit order", "bid-ask spread" or "yield curve". |
| `browse_glossary` | The learning path in order (topics and their terms), or every term in one glossary group. |
| `asset_details` | What one asset is: its market, trading hours, latest official figure or price with source and time, or why no price is shown, plus related terms and its page in the app. |
| `official_figures` | Official figures from free government and regulator sources: US Treasury yields, ECB and Bank of Canada rates, EIA energy prices, and the latest SEC-filed figures (revenue, net income, earnings per share, shares outstanding) for about 30 large US companies and ETFs. Each with its source and date. |
| `crypto_prices` | Prices for the ten major coins, biggest moves first, from `crypto.json` on ATLAS Explain's own data address (captured every 10 minutes by this Worker's cron from Kraken, CoinGecko as backup). Each answer says when they were collected, and how old they are if the refresh has stopped. |
| `price_history` | A coin's start, end, change, high and low over 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, from the same file's 30 days of hourly closes. Past moves only, never a forecast. |
| `todays_headlines` | Recent market headlines from the GDELT Project's news index and official press releases (Federal Reserve, SEC, CFTC, ECB, Bank of Canada, Bank of England), each with its publisher, link and time. News stories older than 36 hours and releases older than 4 days are never returned. |
| `economy_snapshot` | Since 1.6.0. Headline economic figures from `economy.json`, each with period, change and source: US inflation (CPI over 12 months) and unemployment (BLS), the effective fed funds rate with the Fed's target range and SOFR (New York Fed), euro-area inflation (ECB, the `HICP` dataset that replaced `ICP` in Feb 2026), Canada's policy rate (Bank of Canada) and US public debt (Treasury Fiscal Data), about a year of history each, and the next three rate decisions. Optional topic filter. Draws an economy dashboard (`ui://atlas-explain/economy.html`). |
| `economic_calendar` | Since 1.6.0. The next rate decisions of the Fed (FOMC, with the projection meetings marked), the ECB and the Bank of Canada, read from each bank's own calendar page. Dates only; never a guess at the outcome. Optional bank filter. |
| `market_depth` | Since 1.6.0. For one coin: Kraken's best bid and ask (refreshed with the prices), the spread in dollars and basis points, the order book's best 25 levels a side (refreshed by the 10-minute cron), how much sits within 0.1%, 0.5% and 1% of the middle price, and the average price and slippage of filling $1,000, $10,000 and $100,000 against each side. Draws a depth chart (`ui://atlas-explain/market-depth.html`). Explains spreads and slippage; never suggests a trade. |
| `evidence_brief` | Since 1.7.0. One asset on one page, every fact with its source and time: what it is, latest figures or price, the 7-day move and liquidity (coins), the economic backdrop and next rate decision, headlines naming it, terms to know, what people commonly look at, and what the brief does not tell you. Structured output for agents. Never a recommendation, rating or forecast. |
| `upcoming_events` | Since 1.14.0. The next Fed, ECB, Bank of Canada and Bank of England rate decisions (announcement hour in each bank's time zone) and the US CPI, jobs and PPI releases (8:30 am New York, from the BLS iCalendar feed), soonest first, each with a countdown, the latest related figure, and "if this happens" explainers per outcome (`src/core/scenarios.ts`). Never says which outcome will happen. Optional `only`: rates or data. Prompt `whats_coming_up`. |
| `money_maths` | Since 1.13.0. Compound growth (monthly, with monthly additions), what a yearly fee costs over the years, what inflation does to cash, and how a mix's swing falls as equal holdings are added (textbook model with one volatility and one correlation). Pure arithmetic on the person's numbers, limited to sensible ranges; asks for what's missing. Draws a chart with the inputs as fields (`ui://atlas-explain/money-maths.html`), recalculated in the view by the same function (`src/core/money-maths.ts`). |
| `scam_red_flags` | Since 1.13.0. Eleven warning signs of investment fraud as the SEC (Investor.gov) and FINRA describe them, which ones a pasted message shows, where to check registration (BrokerCheck, SEC IAPD, NFA BASIC, aretheyregistered.ca) and report (SEC, FTC, Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre), and recent official fraud actions from the releases already captured. Never says whether something is a scam; the text is not kept. |
| `treasury_auctions` | Since 1.13.0. Announced US Treasury auctions not yet held, and the latest results (yield set, bid-to-cover, share to indirect bidders) from TreasuryDirect, captured with the economy every 6 hours. Optional kind: bills, notes, bonds, TIPS. |
| `compare_countries` | Since 1.12.0. GDP growth, inflation, unemployment and GDP per person for the G20 countries and the EU, from `world.json` (World Bank World Development Indicators through the keyless Data360 API, CC BY 4.0), each with the year of its latest published value. One indicator gives a ranked list; all four give a line per country. Optional country list. Actual values only; a forecast row is dropped when captured. |
| `data_series` | Since 1.16.0. Clean dated rows of official economic data for charts, spreadsheets and reports, with each series' source and licence and the whole answer as CSV: the economy indicators' history (US CPI, unemployment, fed funds, SOFR, debt; euro-area inflation; ECB, Bank of Canada and Bank of England rates) and World Bank series per G20 country (`world_inflation` with countries). Also `world_share_prices` (since 1.20.0): the OECD's monthly share-price index for 17 G20 members, a whole-market measure the OECD allows for any use with credit. Empty lists what's available. Never crypto or individual stock prices (their sources don't allow redistribution). Its view draws a chart per series. Prompt `make_a_report` builds a chart and one-page report from it. |
| `rate_change_impact` | Since 1.17.0 (v2, switched on by the Worker's `V2_TOOLS` variable). What a change in rates the person gives would do to their own variable-rate mortgage payment, HELOC interest, savings interest or GIC/CD renewal, to the cent; Canadian mortgages compound twice a year by default, US monthly. Names the central bank each product follows. Pure arithmetic: never says whether a change will happen. The same function runs the widget at `/embed/rate-impact.html`. |
| `decision_explainer` | Since 1.18.0 (v2); the ECB and the Bank of England since 1.19.0. The Federal Reserve's, Bank of Canada's, ECB's or Bank of England's latest rate decision (or the one before), read from the bank's own statement in `docs.json`: what was decided, how the wording changed from the previous statement (word by word, or sentence by sentence when most of it was rewritten), the bank's own sentences on what it is weighing, and the next scheduled date. Bank of Canada and Bank of England quotes cut to 25 words with links until their reuse terms are confirmed; ECB text reproduced with the ECB as the source, as its notice allows. Never says what comes next. |
| `speech_tracker` | Since 1.18.0 (v2); ECB and Bank of England since 1.19.0. Recent speeches and remarks by Fed, Bank of Canada, ECB or Bank of England policymakers: speaker, title, date, the bank's own one-line description and a link. |
| `notable_today` | Since 1.10.0. What stands out across the data, each item with its source, time and the fixed rule that picked it: a coin whose last 24 hours moved at least 2× its own median daily move over the past month (and at least 1%), a US Treasury curve that is inverted or within 0.25 pt of flat (10-year minus 3-month), a Fed, ECB or Bank of Canada decision within 7 days, an inflation, jobs or rate figure that changed by 0.2 pt or more, and official releases from the last 24 hours. Also lists what was checked and found ordinary, and what couldn't be checked. Draws a card per item with its rule and source (`ui://atlas-explain/notable-today.html`, since 1.11.0). Descriptive only: never a forecast or a suggestion. |

Every answer names its source and ends with: "Educational information, not investment advice."

## Protocol support: dual-era (MCP 2026-07-28 and earlier)

- **2026-07-28 (current, stateless):** no `initialize`; every request carries its version and client capabilities in `_meta`. The server implements the required `server/discover` (supported versions, capabilities, identity, instructions), marks every result `resultType: "complete"` with `io.modelcontextprotocol/serverInfo` in `_meta`, puts `ttlMs`/`cacheScope` on list, read and discover results, and validates the mirrored `MCP-Protocol-Version`, `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers (Base64 sentinel decoded). Errors follow the spec: unsupported version `-32022` with the supported list (HTTP 400), missing client capabilities `-32602` (400), header mismatch `-32020` (400), unknown method `-32601` (HTTP 404), resource not found `-32602`.
- **2025-11-25, 2025-06-18, 2025-03-26, 2024-11-05:** the `initialize` handshake, `ping`, and `-32002` for a missing resource, as those revisions expect.
- **Transport security:** requests from a plain-http page that isn't this computer are refused with 403 (DNS rebinding); https sites and apps are allowed, since the server is public and read-only.
- **Audits:** `test/mcp-modern.test.ts` and `test/mcp-server.test.ts` cover both eras; the live server was checked end to end with a 2026-07-28 client and a legacy client on 15 Nov 2026.

## What else it offers (MCP 2025-11-25 and MCP Apps)

- **Structured output:** every data tool declares an `outputSchema` and returns the same facts as typed JSON in `structuredContent` (for example `priceUsd`, `changePct`, `points`, `sourceUrl`), next to the readable text. Agents and trading-platform assistants can use the fields directly. A test checks every answer against its schema.
- **Interactive charts (MCP Apps, since 1.5.0 three of them):** Claude (web and desktop), ChatGPT and VS Code draw them in a sandboxed frame; clients without MCP Apps show the text answer. They share one frame (`src/mcp/app-shell.ts`: theme, helpers, line chart and the postMessage bridge), load nothing from outside, so the default MCP Apps security policy is enough, and use MIME `text/html;profile=mcp-app`.
  - `price_history` → `ui://atlas-explain/price-chart.html` (`src/mcp/chart-app.ts`): the price line, a readout under the pointer, high and low, the source, and 24H/7D/30D buttons that call `price_history` again through the host.
  - `crypto_prices` → `ui://atlas-explain/crypto-board.html` (`src/mcp/board-app.ts`): every coin with its price, change and a 24-hour sparkline (`spark` in the result); choosing a coin calls `price_history` and opens its chart below.
  - `official_figures` → `ui://atlas-explain/official-figures.html` (`src/mcp/figures-app.ts`): a card per figure (value, change in points for rates, date, source, SEC facts), and a yield curve when the answer holds three or more yields from one source (`tenor` and `change` in the result), with a plain note on whether it slopes up or is inverted.
- **Prompts:** `market_brief`, `explain_asset` (asset), `learn_next` (topic), `brief_on_asset` (asset), `economy_today`, `whats_notable`, `check_an_offer` (offer), `money_lesson` (topic) and `quiz_me` (topic), shown as ready-made starters in clients that support prompts. Each asks for sources and says not to suggest a trade.
- **Claude plugin:** `plugins/atlas-explain` bundles the connector (`.mcp.json`) with four Skills: `learn-a-term`, `market-brief`, `explain-a-move` and `trading-costs`. The repo root's `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` lists it, so `/plugin marketplace add MISHHF93/atlas-explain` installs it in Claude Code. Check it with `claude plugin validate .` (the directory-listing fields show as warnings on older CLIs). `test/plugin.test.ts` keeps its version, connector address and tool names in step with the server; the trade-word scan covers it. To list it in the Claude directory: claude.ai/directory/manage, Submit new, Plugin, this repository.
- **Discovery without connecting:** a server card (name, version, address, no sign-in, every tool and prompt, built from the live lists) at `/.well-known/mcp/server-card.json`, `/.well-known/mcp.json` and `/mcp/server-card`, the paths the server-card drafts (SEP-1649, SEP-2127) and current deployments use; `/health` for uptime monitors. The website also publishes `/llms.txt`.
- **Glossary as resources:** every term is a readable resource (`atlas-explain://glossary/{term}`, Markdown), with a resource template, the learning path (`atlas-explain://learning-path`), and completions for term names, so people can attach a term as context in clients that support resources.
- **Completions:** prompt arguments (coin and catalog names, learning topics) and the glossary template's term names.
- **Icons and annotations:** the server and every tool carry the ATLAS Explain icon, a title, and `readOnlyHint: true`, `destructiveHint: false`, `idempotentHint: true`, as Anthropic's directory review requires.

## What it never does

- It never calls an AI model; the assistant does the talking.
- It never reads accounts, balances or anything on the person's computer.
- It never gives trade advice.

It only reads its built-in glossary and the files this same Worker captures and serves at `/data/v1/` (`market.json`, `news.json`, `crypto.json`).

## How it is hosted ($0)

- **Where:** Cloudflare Workers, free plan: 100,000 requests a day with no card. Over the limit, requests fail for the rest of the day; nothing is ever charged. Firebase can't host it, because the Spark plan has no servers.
- **Transport:** MCP "Streamable HTTP", stateless. Each `POST /mcp` carries one JSON-RPC message and gets one JSON answer; notifications get `202`. `GET /mcp` is `405` (no server stream). `GET /` is a short plain-text description. CORS is open, because the data is public and read-only. It supports protocol versions 2026-07-28 (stateless, no handshake) and 2025-11-25, 2025-06-18, 2025-03-26 and 2024-11-05 (with `initialize`).
- **Caching:** the two data files are cached at Cloudflare's edge for 5 minutes and crypto prices for 1 minute, so heavy use doesn't hit the sources.
- **Own API only:** the server reads nothing but ATLAS Explain's own data (`market.json`, `news.json`, `crypto.json`), which this same Worker captures on a schedule (`src/data/capture.ts`, crons in `wrangler.toml`) and keeps in Workers KV. Its fetch (`src/mcp/live-data.ts`, `ownDataFetch`) reads those files straight from KV and refuses any other address, and a test checks it. `src/data/crypto.ts` is the one coin list, the source parsers and the file reader. This is why the directory's "API ownership" answer is "We own the API".
- **Code:**
  - `src/mcp/server.ts`: the tools and the JSON-RPC handler. They reuse the app's glossary, catalog and headline readers, so the freshness and source rules are the same.
  - `src/mcp/http.ts`: the HTTP transport.
  - `src/mcp/worker.ts`: the Workers entry.
  - `wrangler.toml`: the deploy settings.
- **Checks:** `npm run build:mcp` checks that it bundles for Workers with no Node APIs, and release:check runs it.

## If Claude shows "Couldn't register with ATLAS Explain's sign-in service"

The server has no sign-in. Claude first sends a plain request; if that fails, it falls back to an OAuth sign-in and shows this error. Seen twice on 15 Nov 2026:

1. **Protocol version:** the server returned 400 for `MCP-Protocol-Version: 2025-11-25`, the current spec. Fixed: it accepts 2025-11-25 and any later well-formed version (`src/mcp/http.ts`). Keep this lenient.
2. **Claude kept the old failure:** after the fix, Claude still failed for the plain `/mcp` address without sending a single request (`npx wrangler@4 tail atlas-explain-mcp` showed nothing). The same server under `/mcp?v=2` connected at once. The query is ignored by the server; it only gives Claude a fresh address. If this happens again, delete the connector in Claude and use `?v=3`, then update `MCP_URL`.

3. **Again on 16 Nov 2026, with a different symptom:** the connector showed Connected and listed all seven tools in Customize, its tool-list requests reached the server and succeeded, yet chat said ATLAS Explain "isn't a connected server" and called no tool (it even claimed the sign-in had expired; there is none). Refreshing tools in chat sent no request at all. Removing the connector and adding it again at `/mcp?v=3` (Claude detected **No sign-in** by itself) fixed it at once: a new chat called `crypto_prices` and drew the board. If it happens again, do the same with `?v=4`. Keep `MCP_URL` at the plain `/mcp`: only the owner's account carries the saved failure.

Note: `wrangler tail` did not show Claude's requests even when the tool worked in chat, so an empty tail is not proof that nothing arrived. The test that counts is a chat answer with an `explain_term` tool step.

Sources: [Claude connector troubleshooting](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/troubleshooting), [anthropics/claude-ai-mcp#457](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-ai-mcp/issues/457).

## Owner steps (once)

1. Create a free Cloudflare account (no card) and sign in from this repo: `npx wrangler@4 login`.
2. Deploy: `npm run deploy:mcp`. Wrangler prints the address, for example `https://atlas-explain-mcp.<your-subdomain>.workers.dev`.
3. Send that address to the developer, or set `MCP_URL` in `src/core/links.ts` to it with `/mcp` on the end, then redeploy hosting. The Support page then shows the address and the steps instead of "Not online yet".

After code changes, run `npm run deploy:mcp` again from a computer logged in to Cloudflare. The same deploy updates the data crons.
