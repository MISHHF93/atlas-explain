Set it up

Copy the address, then follow the steps for your assistant. Free, no account and nothing to install.

Connector address
%MCP_URL%
Claude (web and desktop)
  1. Open Customize, then Connectors, then Add connector and Add custom connector.
  2. Name it ATLAS Explain and paste the connector address. Claude detects that no sign-in is needed.
  3. In a chat, turn the connector on and ask, for example: "Using ATLAS Explain, what is a stop-limit order?"
Claude Code

Run this once in a terminal:

claude mcp add --transport http atlas-explain %MCP_URL%
ChatGPT
  1. Turn on developer mode in Settings, under Apps and connectors (available on some plans).
  2. Create a connector, name it ATLAS Explain, paste the connector address, and choose no authentication.
Cursor

Add this to ~/.cursor/mcp.json (or the project's .cursor/mcp.json):

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "atlas-explain": { "url": "%MCP_URL%" }
  }
}
VS Code

Add this to .vscode/mcp.json, or run "MCP: Add Server", choose HTTP and paste the address:

{
  "servers": {
    "atlas-explain": { "type": "http", "url": "%MCP_URL%" }
  }
}
Any other MCP client

Use the connector address with the Streamable HTTP transport and no authentication.

What you can ask it

24 tools and 11 one-click prompts. In Claude, the answers that have numbers open as interactive charts you can explore, with buttons that ask the next question for you.

  • What's notable today?

    Unusual crypto moves, an inverted yield curve, rate decisions this week, figures that just changed, each with its source and the rule that picked it.

  • How is the economy doing?

    Inflation, unemployment, policy rates and US debt from the agencies that publish them, with a year of history and the next rate decisions.

  • How did Bitcoin move this week?

    Ten major coins over 24 hours, 7 or 30 days: start, end, high and low with their times, drawn as a chart.

  • What does a spread cost me?

    A coin's bid, ask and order book, and how far the price moves when filling $1,000, $10,000 or $100,000.

  • Give me the full picture on Apple

    An evidence brief: filed figures, the backdrop, headlines that name it, terms to know, and what the facts don't tell you.

  • Is this offer a scam?

    Paste a message and see which warning signs regulators describe it shows, and where to check and report. It never decides for you.

  • What is $1,000 from 1990 worth today?

    What money from one year buys in another, from the official price index of the US, Canada, the UK or the euro area, back to 1913.

  • Which debt should I pay off first?

    Two methods side by side, highest rate first or smallest balance first, with how long each takes and the interest each costs on your own numbers.

  • What does a 1% fee cost me?

    Compound growth, fees and inflation worked out from your own numbers, drawn as a chart you can change.

  • What would a rate cut do to my mortgage?

    Your own payment, HELOC interest, savings interest or GIC/CD renewal, to the cent, and which central bank drives it. It never says whether rates will change.

  • What did the Bank of Canada just change?

    The latest Fed, Bank of Canada, ECB or Bank of England decision, how the statement's wording changed, word for word, and what the bank says it is watching.

  • What have central bankers been saying?

    Recent speeches by each bank's policymakers: who spoke, when, and a link to the full text. Nothing added or interpreted.

  • Make me a chart of inflation in the G20

    Charts, tables and one-page reports from official series (rates, inflation, growth, share-price indices), each naming its source and licence.

  • Quiz me on interest rates

    A learning path through the glossary, one plain-language term at a time, with short questions to check you've got it.

Explain and learn

Plain-language meanings for market terms, and the learning path topic by topic, from a curated glossary.

Look up an asset

What it is, where and when it trades, and its latest official figure or price, or why no price is shown.

Official figures

US Treasury yields, ECB and Bank of Canada rates, EIA energy prices, and SEC-filed figures for large US companies, each with its source and date.

Crypto prices and charts

Prices for ten major coins, refreshed every few minutes, and how a coin moved over 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, drawn as interactive charts in Claude and ChatGPT. Every answer says when the prices were collected.

The economy

Inflation and unemployment in the US, the euro area, Canada and the UK, each from its own statistics agency; the four central banks' policy rates; the rates people pay (Canada's posted prime, mortgage and GIC rates, US national savings and CD rates, UK fixed mortgage rates, the euro area's home-loan and deposit rates); and US public debt, with about a year of history, and the dates of the next Fed, ECB, Bank of Canada and Bank of England rate decisions.

What a rate change means for you

What a cut or a rise would do to your own variable-rate mortgage, HELOC, savings or GIC/CD renewal, to the cent, and which central bank drives it. Arithmetic on your numbers; it never says whether rates will change.

Central bank decisions, word by word

The latest Fed, Bank of Canada, ECB or Bank of England decision, how the statement's wording changed from the one before, and what the bank says it is watching, quoted with links.

What central bankers are saying

Recent speeches and remarks by each bank's policymakers: who spoke, when, the title and a link to the full text.

Charts and reports

Official series (policy rates, inflation, growth, yields, share-price indices) as data an assistant can turn into a chart, a table or a one-page report, each with its source and licence.

How trading works, with real numbers

A coin's bid-ask spread, its order book as a depth chart, and how far the average price moves when an order is filled: spreads and slippage, explained with live data.

An evidence brief on one asset

Everything above on one page for a coin, a company, a currency or a rate, each fact with its source, plus what the brief doesn't tell you. Facts only, never a recommendation.

Inflation over the years

What money from one year buys in another, from each country's official consumer price index as yearly averages: the US from 1913, Canada from 1914, the UK from 1988 and the euro area from 1997. It describes the past and is not a forecast.

Paying off debts

Several debts and one monthly amount: how long the avalanche (highest rate first) and the snowball (smallest balance first) each take, the interest each costs, and the order debts are cleared. It compares; it never says which to choose.

Money maths

Compound growth, what a fee costs over 30 years, what inflation does to cash, and why holding more things swings less: worked out from your own numbers, drawn as a chart you can change.

Spot an investment scam

Paste a message or offer and see which warning signs regulators describe it shows, where to check the seller is registered and where to report. It never says whether something is a scam.

Treasury auctions

What the US government is borrowing this week, and what its latest auctions paid and how much demand there was, from TreasuryDirect.

Countries compared

GDP growth, inflation, unemployment and GDP per person for the G20 and the EU, from the World Bank, each with the year it was published.

What changed since a date

Every official figure that moved, the central banks' statements and speeches, and the next rate decisions, since any date in the last 90 days, each with its source. Ask for everything or one topic: rates, inflation, jobs, or the US, Canada, the UK or the euro area.

What's notable today

What stands out across all the data: unusual crypto moves, an inverted or flat yield curve, rate decisions this week, figures that just changed, new official releases. Each item says the rule that picked it. It describes; it never forecasts.

Today's headlines

Recent market news and official releases from the Federal Reserve, SEC, CFTC, ECB, Bank of Canada and Bank of England, each linked to its publisher.

Built to be trusted

  • Education only. No predictions. It explains and shows sources. It never recommends a trade or says where a price is going.
  • No made-up numbers. Missing data stays missing, with the reason. Every figure names its source and time.
  • Never your accounts. It never reads balances, account numbers, positions or other pages, and never sees the other connectors in your assistant. Anything you do in another service happens there, under its own terms.
  • Private. The connector only receives what your assistant asks it, changes nothing and stores none of it: it keeps only daily totals of how often each tool is used. See how the connector handles data.
  • Open data. Developers can read the same published files, and what each source permits, at the data index. For information and education, not for transactions.

Questions

How do I add it to my AI assistant?

Add one address as a connector in Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, VS Code or any assistant that supports MCP. The address and the steps for each assistant are on Set it up. No account or key is needed.

Is it free?

Yes. The connector, explaining, learning and live prices are free. A Pro plan with more cloud answers in the web app may be offered later, and you will always see the price before paying anything.

Does it give trading advice?

No. It is education only. It explains terms and shows sources, and it never tells anyone to trade or says where a price is going.

What does the connector receive?

Only what your assistant sends when it uses an ATLAS Explain tool, such as a term or a ticker. It never sees your chat history, accounts or files, changes nothing and keeps none of it.

Where do the figures and prices come from?

Official figures come from government and regulator sources, crypto prices from a public market-data provider, and headlines from public news feeds. Each answer names its source and time. Missing data stays missing, with the reason.

Can I use it without an AI assistant?

Yes. The web app at atlas-explain.web.app/app explains whatever you type or paste. In desktop Chrome it tries Gemini Nano on your device first; otherwise Gemini answers in the cloud. Every answer says which one ran.

Do I need an account?

For the web app, yes: a free account, with Google or a one-time email link. It keeps your saved explanations and settings on every device. The AI-assistant connector needs no account.

If something looks stuck

  • Your assistant can't reach ATLAS Explain: check the address on Set it up, then remove the connector and add it again.
  • "Cloud" answers do not arrive in the web app: check your connection. Each browser has a fair-use limit of 8 cloud answers a minute and 300 a day; on-device answers are never limited.
  • Answers always say "Cloud": Gemini Nano needs recent desktop Chrome on a supported computer. When it isn't available, the cloud answers instead and says so.
  • The site looks out of date: reload with Ctrl+Shift+R (Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

Something not working, or a term explained badly? Open an issue (never include account numbers or anything private). To see or delete your account data, open Account in the web app, or read the privacy policy.
